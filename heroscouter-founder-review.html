[ The problem ]

Today, our candidate pool lives in one person's head.

When a role lands, finding a fit depends on Purvesh remembering who we've spoken to. What we learn about each candidate is spread across places we can't search or match.

Where candidate knowledge lives today
Purvesh's memoryCan't be searched or shared
Fathom callsRecorded, rarely reused
ResumesEvery one in a different format
Email and LinkedInScattered across threads
What it costs us
  • Slow shortlistsWe search from scratch for roles where a fit already exists.
  • Forgotten fitsGood candidates sit unnoticed in our own pool.
  • Candidates go coldSomeone we didn't place in month 1 is hard to reach in month 4.
  • A ceiling on growthThe business can only hold what one person remembers.

The root cause is how we capture what we learn. Matching only works once every candidate is recorded the same way. So the engine starts by building profiles from the calls and resumes we already have, with no typing.

[ Tool and people ]

The tool remembers and drafts. People do the talking.

You asked what belongs in the tool, what should be automated, and what stays manual. This is the split.

  • Tailored resumes and masked profiles
  • Submissions, stages and outcomes
  • The public open-roles page, later fed from role records

[ Risks and tests ]

What could go wrong, and how we find out early.

Three things could make this a wasted build. We test the two biggest with no code, while the developer builds the capture step that we need either way.

Nobody uses it

The trap you described: tools that look good and get zero adoption.

Profiles build themselves from Fathom calls, and the weekly list arrives without logging in. If we're still keeping side sheets at the second checkpoint, we fix that before adding anything.
AI matches are no better than memory

If the engine only suggests people Purvesh would think of anyway, it adds little.

Test 1: a blind matching test before we build matching.
Too few open roles

If roles are scarce, better matching won't move placements. More clients will.

Test 2: pitch real candidates to companies already hiring for their profile.
Test 1 · Matching by handPurvesh + Vandit
Part 1: Do our calls hold enough?Run 10 recent intake calls and resumes through a prompt. Check that stack, years, pay, location, work authorization and notice period come out.
Part 2: A blind testFor each open role, Purvesh sees 7 names: the AI's top 5 plus 2 random people from the pool, unlabeled. He marks each as approach or skip.
PassAI picks get approved clearly more often than random ones, and at least one is someone he wouldn't have recalled.
FailRandom picks score as well as AI picks. Matching adds no signal yet.

Decides how we build matching, and which fields intake calls must always cover.

Test 2 · Candidate-led outreachPurvesh + Vandit
Pick 5 strong candidatesPeople we'd be proud to put in front of any client.
Find who's hiring for themAn X-ray search by hand finds about 10 companies hiring for each profile.
Pitch, personallyA short email with a masked profile, and one follow-up.
Pass3 or more replies and at least 1 client call from about 50 emails.
Fail0 or 1 replies.

Decides whether outreach moves into the first release.

[ Not doing yet ]

What we're leaving out, and why.

Not in this product, for now
  • Portals for candidates or clientsThey get outputs from a person, not a login.
  • Automated messagesThey would break the personal touch.
  • Notifications for every new recordLow value while we create records by hand. They return once calls create them.
  • Inbox and WhatsApp syncLogging a contact takes one step. Sync is costly and invasive.
  • Referrals and communityThey need placements first. They're in Later.
Parked as separate tracks
  • SEO and AEO foundationsSitemap, pages and CMS first. Results take months, so it runs on its own track.
  • A daily US-company digest for the teamA short scheduled read on US companies and the hiring landscape. A prompt, not product.
  • Free recruiter toolsSmall tools like an X-ray search builder that bring people to our site.
